rollerskating

[pics of my rollerskates]

i actually didn't get into rollerskating this summer, but it was actually around late april that i picked up my rollerskates again. the amount of motivation you need to have in order to make progress in something like this is honestly astronomical. but i can't even lie: i love rollerskating. it's really hard for someone that just knows how to roll around, but there's this feeling of freedom that it gives me every time i put on those skates. as someone that spends most of their day sitting in a desk, it is crucial for me to have a hobby that involves moving my body. to me that is what rollerskating is to me.

ever since i got my new job, i've been finding it hard to go rollerskating. i feel physically tired when i get out of work, or sometimes i just feel like i don't have much time left in the day.

web stuff: doqmeat, comms, work, etc.

i did so much this summer in terms of web dev dev that during late july (and even now i can still feel it) i got really burntout from the usual fucking around with HTML that i like to do... so during july i didn't really do a lot with my site's rework. and that's okay. i am at least very happy i got to finish the ~new~ layouts like this very journal and the gaming log during this summer.

[pic of figma gaming log prototype]

having an actual job related to this field is also kinda crazy. at first i was really nervous about it since i didn't really know a lot about backend or django but i did eventually get the hang of it. most off my tasks are related to the front end of the website but funnily enough i spent a whole lot more of time on the back end figuring how the data was getting sent over. you see the thing about my web dev job is that the project i am currently working on has been worked on by many students before me. students that are no longer working on said project. and SO! i also spend a lot of my time understanding old code written by many different people. which i guess is nothing crazy. but the many times something wasn't working because it was hard coded on the javascript was headache inducing during the first 2 times. now whenever something doesn't work, i always make sure to check the javascript files first.

besides my sad burntout, i did learn a lot about web dev during july. even from just doing my silly little comms. i managed to dip my toes into a hosting service that allows me dynamic sites, meaning i get to write my own back end AND front end, which i am currently using for sepiarecord.net. right now it's just a simple index page but i did get started with setting up the siinamota fanlisting there by importing all of the existing members into the database, having different tables for the siinamota albums and countries, managing to display the members, and making the form page. the fanlisting as of right now has a tedious workflow of having to input each member manually. so now with a database that should make things way easier, though it will take me a bit to build it using PHP and MySQL. but at least my databases final project prepared me for this.
